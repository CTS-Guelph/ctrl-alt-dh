Going into this project I thought that my personality was reflected through trends on my Instagram Explore page. However, after recording my interactions over 5 days and reviewing how I interacted with the content, it was clear that even the interactions I felt were unimportant were all used to curate my digital identity on Instagram. Each pause, view and like, along with what I select on my curated Instagram Explore page further develops how the platform sees me and is then able to further target products and content in hopes of getting me to buy something or track my habits to hopefully sell me something in the future.

In my Twine game, I recorded manual statistics from 5, 20-minute interactions with my Instagram Explore page. Although I could have adjusted the scope of this project differently and recorded changes in my digital personality by requesting my data from Meta, I wanted to try and put myself in the shoes of the algorithm and see how I engaged with the content. By screen recording each session I was able to re-watch the content I scrolled through somewhat mindlessly. I realized firsthand how my knowledge and interests had shifted over a week. Although my before and after screenshots were mainly related to fashion and bicycles it was interesting to see how my feed became flooded by that content and see the number of interactions, such as posts I liked and how many I watched in that period. Along with my interests being further interested in the subject throughout the week and even currently.

As mentioned in the article [Know Thyself: Tracking Every Facet of Life, from Sleep to Mood to Pain](https://www.wired.com/2009/06/lbnp-knowthyself), just like how we had turned telescopes back towards ourselves into microscopes to better understand what makes us the humans we are. We are now combining our data with others through group insights. Seeing how not only one person is being surveyed, such as the idea of being viewed under a microscope for all aspects of our data by platforms. Instead, we are seeing how mass media trends and information are affecting larger groups of people and changing digital and physical personalities under what the article references as a macroscope. The content I view especially in the example of fashion, I am being fed a certain style which also influences others with a similar taste. The larger the following in a certain niche that platforms like Instagram or Meta pick up, they will then be able to better match user interests and predict how groups will react in the future based on popularity. Thus, furthering the idea of being surveyed and how our interactions are being predicted before you know what they are, since we also continue to share ideas which act as a representation of who we are and how we have been influenced.

This led me to think about [Michel Foucault’s concept of the panopticon](https://monoskop.org/images/4/43/Foucault_Michel_Discipline_and_Punish_The_Birth_of_the_Prison_1977_1995.pdf) which I see relevant in my project through a digital lens. Platforms are increasingly putting out content to users through their algorithms which are keeping them engaged for longer periods. Though one might feel as though they are alone when exploring the platform, all their actions are being monitored. This may create actual consequences based on actions against the platform guidelines. An unsettling aspect which funds the platform is the fact that users are increasingly carving out more of their time to be in a place where their actions are being monitored to make it more appealing. When doing this self-analysis, I put myself not only in the position of a “prisoner” to social media but also a guard, watching my actions and perceiving how they might change my experience later as the algorithm continues to confine me and keep me entertained by seeing what I do and don’t engage with.

Of course, this data project quantifies and qualitatively displays a reflection of my Instagram interactions, and it is limited only to how I perceive and act on the app. Though it is inferred that what I am truly interested in are the things I will engage with on Instagram it is completely possible to portray a different identity on the app and persuade the app to curate a false digital identity. I think it is accurate to say that currently my feed is developing into an array of my more popular interests, yet the app does not capture a true mirror of my social identity, but one that is close.

When creating this project with Twine I was inspired by the self-surveillance of [Kenneth Goldsmith’s Soliliquey](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/goldsmith__soliloquy/days.html). In his case, he used a tape recorder to record every spoken word of his throughout the week. I think that the action of recording the actions I take on social media holds a related value. Though my interactions with the platform are primarily private, the footprint of my interactions can be seen by other users. Such as my posts, comments, likes, and stories. This is a way in which it is different than Goldsmith’s project, as spoken word may have an impact on those nearby if they are listening in, though only if it’s recorded can it be replayed. Whereas, the digital footprint of interactions stays, and it is easier for things to be saved, screenshotted or screen recorded.

This project is a way to invade and make visible a slice of my interactions with Instagram. It is an active reminder to myself of how I portray my digital self. Does it mirror who I am in reality? Knowing that both the algorithm and other users can see my interactions, authenticity has become increasingly important to me as I take on the risks of sharing who I am on the platform. By quantifying my data and visualizing aspects of how my information informs future content that is shown to me; this counter-surveillance project has been a positive reflection for me to be conscious of how I manage my life on the platform and better understand its ability to cater to my interests and my awareness of its addictive nature.
