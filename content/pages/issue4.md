                    Social Media & Youth: The Well-Being of the Self
                </a>
            </h3>
            <p>Rebecca Logan</p>
            <p>Social Media & Youth and The Well-Being of the Self is a web essay that explores the impact of social media on youth, with a particular focus on identity and mental well-being. Written around the keyword Identity, the article looks at the relationship between the digital self and the actual self, as well as how social media can influence the way young people see themselves. I chose this topic because it is something I find personally interesting and felt there were many different aspects worth exploring. I chose the web essay format because it allowed me to explore the topic in depth. However, one of my biggest challenges was adjusting to a less formal and more creative style of writing. I am more comfortable with traditional, structured essays, so I found myself approaching the web essay in a similar way rather than fully experimenting with the format. If I were to develop the submission further, I would like to make it more interactive and creative. The article connects to the theme Navigating Digital Anxieties by exploring some of the pressures social media can create around identity, self-image, comparison, and mental well-being. It considers how online experiences can influence the way youth understand themselves both online and offline.</p>

            <h3>
                <a href="{filename}/posts/issue4-13.md">
                    The Impact of External Pressure on Cybersecurity Decision Making
                </a>
            </h3>
            <p>Thomas Hendriks</p>
            <p>This project explores how external pressures and distractions influence cybersecurity decision-making. Three serious games were built using the A.I. platform Base44, while researching serious games, simulation-based design, cybersecurity, and human behaviour. Each game placed players in a cybersecurity scenario in which they had to deal with built-in distractions: Managing passwords while being distracted by the manager, identifying email scams while driving, and dealing with computer viruses while supervising a child. User feedback was collected to see how these pressures affect focus, memory, and decision-making. By combining cybersecurity education with simulated everyday distractions, this project looks into how the pressures of digital and physical environments can influence cybersecurity behaviour and contribute to the anxiety people experience in a digital world.</p>
        </div>
